Index coverage

Not all journals in Pinakes are indexed the same way, and not all relevant publishers are indexed at all. This page documents what's fully covered, what's partially covered, and what's missing; it also explains why these gaps exist at the level of infrastructure rather than curatorial choice.

The co-authorship network, citation graph, citation trend charts, Most Cited rankings, topic heatmap, and institution visualizations on the Explore page are all built from the same underlying data, so venues with partial coverage contribute less to them than their presence in the field would warrant.

If you read nothing else on this page

Three things to keep in mind before you read anything on the Explore page:

  1. {% if tiers %}{{ tiers.counts.with_refs }} of the {{ tiers.counts.indexed }} indexed journals have reference lists in the index for at least half of their articles.{% else %}Only some of the indexed journals have their reference lists in the index.{% endif %} Citation networks, centrality scores, and coupling and co-citation views underrepresent the rest: they show what has been deposited, not what has been written.
  2. Some journals can be cited but cannot cite. If a venue has no DOIs, its references aren't deposited anywhere Pinakes can read them. Older content, especially pre-2000 NCTE journals, has the same problem at scale: most NCTE volumes before 2000 have no machine-readable reference lists at all.
  3. Book chapters are partially indexed. This particularly affects writing-across-the-curriculum, writing-center, and edited-collection-heavy sub-areas, where a substantial share of the field's argumentative work lives in monographs rather than journals. The networks underweight that scholarship.

The rest of this page is the detailed accounting behind those three claims.

Two tiers of indexing

Pinakes draws from two kinds of sources, and which kind supplies a journal's records determines what its articles contribute to citations, author affiliations, topic tagging, and the visualizations on the Explore page.

Full CrossRef indexing means a journal registers each article's metadata (title, authors, abstract, publication date, reference list) with CrossRef using a registered DOI. This enables: citation tracking (because CrossRef carries reference lists), OpenAlex enrichment (institutional affiliations, topic classifications, open-access status), and reliable author disambiguation. Most commercial publishers and NCTE do this for current content.

RSS or scrape-only indexing means Pinakes collects basic article metadata (title, authors, publication date) from an RSS feed or by parsing the journal's website. These articles appear in search results and the timeline, but they contribute nothing to the citation network, and OpenAlex enrichment is hit-or-miss without a DOI. Author affiliation data for these articles is largely absent.

Coverage by sub-area

The cluster scheme used in the Atlas of the Field and the journal sidebar maps roughly onto distinct coverage profiles. Reading the index by sub-area rather than by individual journal helps locate where the citation networks overweight some scholarship and underweight others.

  • Composition & Writing Studies — Strong CrossRef coverage on the flagship NCTE journals (CCC, College English, RTE) for current content, with the well-known era gap before 2000 where back-catalog content lives in JSTOR without article-level DOIs. TETYC is CrossRef-deposited, but only about one article in eight has a reference list. Composition Studies and Basic Writing e-Journal are scraped from their own sites. The Journal of Basic Writing has DOIs for its whole run (1975 to 2025) and no reference lists. Journal of Response to Writing is OAI-PMH harvested from BYU's bepress repository: titles, authors, and abstracts, with no DOIs and no reference lists. Discourse and Writing / Rédactologie (Canadian, open access) and Academic Labor: Research and Artistry have DOIs and no reference lists; Written Language & Literacy has reference lists for fewer than half of its records. Citation networks should read this cluster's pre-2000 scholarship as systematically underweighted.
  • Writing Research & Assessment — Assessing Writing has reference lists for about four articles in five, and the Journal of Second Language Writing for about seven in ten of its CrossRef records. Journal of Writing Research and Journal of Academic Writing are CrossRef-indexed with no reference lists deposited. The Journal of Writing Assessment has DOIs from 2021 on, with abstracts and no reference lists; its earlier volumes have no DOIs and are not in the index.
  • Rhetoric — Strong coverage on the major venues (Rhetoric Society Quarterly, Rhetoric Review, Philosophy & Rhetoric, Advances in the History of Rhetoric, Written Communication). Rhetoric & Public Affairs and Rhetorica deposit reference lists for about one article in five and one in six. Quarterly Journal of Speech (1915 to the present) and Argumentation and Advocacy carry rhetorical studies as the communication discipline practices it; both have reference lists for part of their records, and the older QJS volumes have none. Women's Studies in Communication sits beside Peitho, and about half its records have reference lists. Peitho, Poroi, and Res Rhetorica are CrossRef-indexed with no reference lists. RhetNet (1994 to 1997) is an early online journal with 32 records and no reference lists. Pre/Text is manually indexed (analog only); JAC is manually indexed from CompPile records (1980–2014; a subscription journal that never deposited DOIs); Enculturation, Present Tense, and KB Journal are RSS-only and contribute no outbound citations.
  • Technical Communication — The most densely indexed cluster in the corpus. TCQ, JBTC, JTWC, BPCQ, CDQ, and Double Helix all have reference lists for seventy percent or more of their articles. IEEE Transactions on Professional Communication has them for about a third, mostly recent volumes, and the Communication Design Quarterly Review for few. Rhetoric of Health and Medicine is CrossRef-indexed but deposits no reference lists. This is the cluster where citation-network analyses are most reliable; the in-progress Datastories project uses it as its primary corpus for that reason. Technical Communication, the STC journal, is not in the index; see the list of journals not yet indexed below.
  • Writing Centers — Mixed. WLN: A Journal of Writing Center Scholarship has registered DOIs since 2015, but deposits them without an ISSN or reference lists, so Pinakes doesn't yet index its current run, and its earlier run as Writing Lab Newsletter is indexed only issue by issue. Writing Center Journal is scraped from Purdue's Digital Commons (titles and authors), Praxis from its own site, and The Peer Review (IWCA) from the IWCA site; none of the three contributes reference lists. Citation analyses underweight writing-center scholarship overall, and pre-2010 writing-center work is especially sparse.
  • WAC / Writing Across the Curriculum — Across the Disciplines has full CrossRef coverage with reference lists. The WAC Journal is CrossRef-indexed with none. Language and Learning Across the Disciplines (1994 to 2003) and Academic.Writing (2000 to 2003), the WAC Clearinghouse's earlier journals, have DOIs and no reference lists. The bigger coverage limitation here is structural: a substantial share of WAC scholarship lives in monographs and edited collections from WAC Clearinghouse Press and other WAC-friendly publishers, and those books are only partially indexed. Citation networks systematically underweight WAC's monograph-based argumentative work.
  • Digital & Multimodal — Asymmetric. Computers and Composition is CrossRef-indexed, with reference lists for about two articles in three. Kairos is born-digital and webtext-native, indexed via custom scraping; its articles appear as nodes but contribute no outbound citation edges, because their reference lists are not deposited. Journal of Multimodal Rhetorics has minimal CrossRef compliance. The cluster's central venue is well-indexed but the multimodal-webtext side is structurally invisible to citation-derived analyses.
  • Community Literacy — Community Literacy Journal is CrossRef-indexed (2001 to 2022) but deposits no reference lists. Reflections, RSS-only until spring 2026, now has full CrossRef coverage: Penn State Libraries deposited its complete metadata and reference lists in May 2026, and the journal contributes citation edges. As with Writing Centers and WAC, much of the cluster's scholarly conversation lives in books and edited collections that are partially indexed.

Journals: partial or limited coverage

These journals are in the index but contribute reduced data to the visualizations. The technical reason varies by venue.

Manually indexed print journals — no DOIs

These journals exist only as print-era records: no DOIs, no machine-readable reference lists, no web presence (or no machine-readable web presence). Coverage comes from hand-compiled article indexes or secondary bibliographic sources. Records include title, author, volume/issue, and date; some include keywords and annotations.

  • Pre/Text: A Journal of Rhetorical Theory (ed. Victor J. Vitanza, 1980–2016) — 234 articles across volumes 1–22; volumes 19–20 not indexed. The journal was never digitized.
  • JAC: A Journal of Rhetoric, Culture, and Politics (originally JAC: Journal of Advanced Composition, 1980–2014) — 1,180 articles across volumes 1–34, sourced from CompPile records. JAC was a subscription print journal that ceased active publication around 2014 and never deposited DOIs. Records include CompPile's keyword vocabulary and annotations (annotations present for about 1% of entries). Book reviews are tagged as such.

RSS or scrape only — no CrossRef DOIs

For these journals, Pinakes collects title, author, and date from RSS feeds, OAI-PMH endpoints, or the journal's own web pages, so their articles carry no citation data, reference lists, or OpenAlex enrichment.

  • Kairos: A Journal of Rhetoric, Technology, and Pedagogy — custom HTML format; scraped manually per issue
  • Enculturation — RSS only
  • Composition Forum — RSS only
  • Present Tense: A Journal of Rhetoric in Society — RSS only
  • KB Journal (Kenneth Burke Society) — RSS only
  • Journal of Response to Writing (BYU ScholarsArchive, bepress) — OAI-PMH harvest. Full archive (2015–present) with titles, authors, and abstracts; no DOIs. Strong candidate for the DOI-deposit outreach pattern Reflections completed with Penn State Libraries in May 2026.
  • WPA: Writing Program Administration (Council of Writing Program Administrators) — full run (1977–present, 103 issues) indexed from the CWPA archive page. Titles and authors throughout; no abstracts. The journal has never registered a DOI, so it contributes no citation edges. DOI-registration outreach to the incoming editorial team is in progress.
  • Writing Lab Newsletter — 354 issue-level records scraped from full-issue PDFs, running through 2013; no DOIs, abstracts, or reference lists. The journal's current run, WLN: A Journal of Writing Center Scholarship (volume 40 on, 2015–present), has DOIs from the WAC Clearinghouse, but they are deposited without an ISSN or reference lists, so Pinakes doesn't yet index it.

Small journals without CrossRef registration

These are in the index through scraping. Their publishers never completed CrossRef membership or registered DOIs, so the content is open access but invisible to APIs.

  • Basic Writing e-Journal — static HTML on the CUNY site; some volumes are missing
  • The Peer Review (IWCA) — writing center journal; limited metadata structure

CrossRef registration incomplete by era

These journals have CrossRef records for part of their run. The rest is either on JSTOR without article-level DOIs or was never registered.

  • College Composition and Communication — anything before roughly 1999 has no machine-readable article-level metadata despite being in JSTOR
  • Journal of Writing Assessment — DOIs begin in 2021. Earlier volumes have none and are not in the index.

Hosted platforms without CrossRef

  • Journal of Multimodal Rhetorics — newer journal; minimal CrossRef compliance at time of indexing
  • Literacy in Composition Studies — OA; DOIs registered under prefix 10.21623 but ISSN linkage was incomplete until recently. Reference lists are scraped from HTML galleys rather than deposited with CrossRef.

Journals not in the index

Journals the field reads that Pinakes does not yet carry, and why. Each is a separate piece of work.

  • English Journal (NCTE) — registered with CrossRef, about 26,000 records, mostly K–12 teaching. Held back for scope; it would add that many records to the index.
  • Technical Communication (Society for Technical Communication) — CrossRef holds only 57 of its records, 2022 to 2024. The back catalog would need a scraper.
  • Journal of Teaching Writing and Journal of Contemporary Rhetoric — not in CrossRef. Each would need its own scraper.
  • Reading and Writing and English Education — registered with CrossRef; held back for scope. The first leans toward cognitive psychology, the second toward teacher education.
  • Young Scholars in Writing — undergraduate research journal; OJS, no CrossRef.
  • Xchanges — technical communication open-access journal; OJS with minimal CrossRef registration.
  • Rhetor (Canadian Society for the Study of Rhetoric) — its domain was taken over in early 2025 and now redirects to an unrelated site; the journal's status is unknown.

Books: university presses with gaps

The books index covers about 800 titles from seven publishers, along with chapter-level records for their edited collections. The gaps in book coverage stem from a structural problem distinct from the journal situation: CrossRef's data model was designed around journals, where a container (journal title + ISSN) gives you a reliable handle on a series. For books, the equivalent would be the series ISSN, a standard that exists but that most publishers either don't register or don't include in their CrossRef deposits. This means you can't query CrossRef for "all books in X series" the way you can query for "all articles in X journal."

Series metadata missing from CrossRef

These publishers have DOI-registered books but don't include series names in CrossRef, making their relevant series undiscoverable by automated query. Coverage in Pinakes comes from manually curated title lists and direct ISBN lookups.

  • Routledge (Taylor & Francis) — four major series relevant to the field (Routledge Studies in Rhetoric and Communication, Routledge Research in Writing Studies, ATTW Series in Technical and Professional Communication, Routledge Studies in Technical Communication, Rhetoric, and Culture) contain roughly 96 titles. CrossRef series queries return approximately 26. The remainder require individual lookup by known title.
  • University of Alabama Press — publishes Studies in Rhetoric and Communication, a significant series. Books have individual DOIs but the series isn't queryable.
  • SUNY Press — SUNY Series on Literacy, Culture, and Learning and adjacent series; same problem.
  • Fordham University Press — some rhetoric titles with DOIs but no series tagging.
  • University of Michigan Press — digital rhetoric titles; CrossRef records exist but series metadata is absent.
  • University of South Carolina Press — rhetoric and public address titles.
  • University of Nevada Press — place-based rhetoric and composition titles.

Publishers with minimal CrossRef footprint

  • Hampton Press — now closed; published significant rhet/comp scholarship through the 2000s–2010s under the Cresskill imprint. Most titles have no DOIs. A curated ISBN list would be the only route to indexing these.
  • Peter Lang — publishes rhet/comp-adjacent work in applied linguistics and L2 writing; DOI registration is inconsistent across imprints.
  • Multilingual Matters — significant for L2 writing research; CrossRef records exist but are not discoverable by series, and their website returns 403 to automated requests.
  • Equinox Publishing — applied linguistics and discourse studies overlap; minimal CrossRef series data.
  • Cambridge University Press and Oxford University Press — both have DOIs for relevant titles, but their relevant series aren't queryable by series via CrossRef. Full coverage would require a manually compiled title list.

What this means for the visualizations

The analytical tools on the Explore page are built entirely from the indexed data. Coverage gaps affect each visualization differently:

  • Publication timeline — Journals indexed via RSS or scrape appear in the timeline but may have gaps where issues weren't captured. Pre-2000 back catalog is sparse for most journals.
  • Topic co-occurrence heatmap — Tags are drawn from a 61-term disciplinary vocabulary matched against title and abstract text, and 63% of indexed articles carry at least one (34,191 of 54,251 in September 2026). Most untagged articles have no abstract (69%), and just over half predate 1990. College English and CCC account for about half of them; many of the older College English pieces are literary criticism, poetry, and fiction, which fall outside the vocabulary. Articles with an abstract are tagged 77% of the time, against 48% for articles without one.
  • Author network — Co-authorship links depend on articles being in the index. Authors who publish primarily in venues with partial coverage will appear less connected than they actually are. Solo authors may appear as isolates even if they have collaborative work in unindexed venues.
  • Citation network — Only journals that deposit reference lists with CrossRef contribute citation edges. See the coverage table on the Citations tab for per-journal detail. RSS-only journals contribute zero citation edges regardless of how frequently they cite or are cited.
  • Institutions — Affiliation data comes from OpenAlex, which requires a DOI to look up. Articles without DOIs have no institution data and don't appear in the institutions visualization.

The timeline, topic heatmap, author network, institutions, and citation-trends panels take a From / To publication-year range, as the citation views already did. Use it when one long-running venue would otherwise set the scale: Quarterly Journal of Speech alone reaches back to 1915.
